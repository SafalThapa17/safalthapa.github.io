I work at the intersection of data, economics and agriculture. My question is simple: how can better information help farmers, firms and governments make better decisions about food, storage and markets?

Before graduate school, I co-founded and ran a post-harvest storage business in Nepal for four years. We stored vegetable seeds and other commodities for farmers and traders. That experience showed me how much of a farmer's income depends on decisions made after harvest: when to sell, whether to store, and how to manage spoilage and price risk. It also showed me how little reliable data exists to guide those decisions.

I hold a B.S. in Computer Science and an M.S. in Data Science and Engineering from City University of New York, City College. I use these skills as tools to study economic and social questions, not as ends in themselves.

Research interests
Post-harvest storage and price risk: how storage access changes when farmers sell and who captures the gains
Small food enterprises in value chains: what limits the growth of storage, cold-chain and seed businesses in South Asia
Financial inclusion for farmers: how credit, storage-backed lending and digital finance shape agricultural decisions
Data for public decisions: whether better data changes how governments allocate resources and deliver services
