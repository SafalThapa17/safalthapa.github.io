I co-founded and ran a post-harvest storage business for four years, storing vegetable seeds and other commodities for farmers and traders.

**What we did**
- Provided storage that let farmers and traders hold produce instead of selling at harvest-time prices
- [Add: number of farmers served, storage capacity, commodities, or another concrete figure]

**What I learned**
- Much of a farmer's income depends on decisions made *after* harvest: when to sell, whether to store, and how to manage spoilage and price risk.
- Reliable data to guide those decisions rarely exists.

**Building with data**
I built an operations system (Flask, SQLite, Chart.js) to run the business: inventory, purchases, sales, expenses, loss tracking, storage rent and reports. It turned paper records into data on every lot that entered and left storage, which showed which commodities spoiled, how long stock sat, and what storage actually earned.

These questions now drive my research interests in storage, price risk and farmer welfare.
